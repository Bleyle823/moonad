In 2024 I interned at the National ICT Innovation and Integration Centre for three months. I finished that attachment on good terms, and I still have good relations with a number of people at the Centre. I am writing to people who already know me, to ask NI3C to recognise **MOONAD** as an innovation for teaching and learning, to see a demonstration, to advise on how a class could use it, and to consider it for incubation or an innovations fair.

MOONAD is a rover in a computer model of the Moon. It drives around rocks, finds a sample, and returns to a landing pad. Each step is written down: the rover started, it travelled, it found something, it delivered it. A class can watch that job the way they would watch a lab practical. Payment, and a share for the people who funded the rover, are the next layer. The lesson comes first.

Kenya Space Agency already trains students on small satellites and on reading satellite data. MOONAD is a graduate project in that spirit: a machine a pupil can see working, with the limits said out loud. The Agency has not adopted it. The Centre would be looking at the learning use.

## What a class would see

A short lesson can follow one trip.

- The rover comes online on a grey landscape and plans a path.
- It slows or stops when the ground ahead is unclear.
- It recognises a sample and drives back to the pad.
- A log on screen lists the four marks of the job.

Pupils also hear the limit. A signal to the Moon takes more than a second, so the rover decides its own turns. The public record, when it is attached, is the receipt. It does not steer.

## Why a classroom, and why now

The Kenya Space Agency was established in 2017 to promote, coordinate, and regulate space activities for national development. Kenya has flown small satellites, including 1KUNS-PF and Taifa-1. In February 2025 the Agency trained students on CubeSats with Multimedia University of Kenya. In January 2026 it joined an Earth-observation hackathon at the University of Nairobi.

Those programmes ask young people to touch the work. MOONAD is a way to do that on a laptop: one rover, one job, one log. A live payment and a rover on the Moon come after the lesson is clear. This letter is my request to the Centre where I interned.

The aims are a lesson a teacher can run, advice from the Centre on age and length, and, if the idea holds, a path toward incubation. The letter stays with the demonstration.

## How the record is kept

The landscape is a simulation. The rover writes a local log of the trip. peaq is the network where that log is meant to become a public record, so a later buyer can check the job before paying. The simulation does not yet send that transaction. A class sees the log. The wallet, when it exists, stays off the pupils' screen.

People on Earth who fund a later rover would share what it earns. Selling those shares to the public waits on legal advice. The classroom version shows the job.
