Dear ETHSafari organisers,

I have been part of this community for a while. I have taken part in the ETHSafari hackathons, and I was at the first ETHSafari. I am writing as a member, asking you to recognise **MOONAD** as a Kenyan use of Web3 for a machine that works far from its owners, and to hold a short demo or a startup booth for it when the next Safari dates are confirmed. The 2026 edition was postponed, and new dates are still to be announced. This letter waits for those dates.

MOONAD is a rover that does a job, gets paid when the job is confirmed, and shares the earnings with people on Earth who helped fund it. In the project today the rover lives in a computer model of the Moon. It drives, finds a sample, and returns to a landing pad. The trip is logged. A public identity and a live payment are the next steps. A rover on the Moon is further off. The demo is the job you can watch.

A message to the Moon takes more than a second, so the rover drives itself. The public record is the receipt. It follows the work.

## Why a Safari audience should care

Kenya already has a space agency and small satellites of its own. The Kenya Space Agency, established in 2017, promotes and regulates space activity for national development. Kenya has flown 1KUNS-PF and Taifa-1. The Agency trains students. MOONAD is a graduate project from a BBIT alumnus of Africa Nazarene University, class of 2024, in that same line of learning. The Agency has not adopted it.

The Web3 use is a machine whose work can be checked by people who will never stand next to it, and whose earnings can be shared with the people who paid to build it. This letter is my own request.

The aims are a demo of one job, a public record for that job on a test network, and a later ground test on imitation lunar soil. The letter stays with those aims.

## Identity, the job, and the share

What a visitor sees today is a simulation: grey ground, rocks, a six-wheeled rover, a landing pad. The rover writes a local log: it came online, it travelled, it found a sample, it delivered the sample. peaq is the network where that log is meant to become a public record tied to the rover. The simulation does not yet send that transaction. The demo screen will say so.

When a buyer pays, the payment waits for the confirmed job. The share for funders on Earth is agreed with them first. The percentages stay blank in this letter. Selling shares to the public waits on legal advice. The wheels stay with the rover.

## Why this belongs at a Safari

From the first Safari through the hackathons, the useful sessions have been the ones that show a working use. A rover job with a receipt is that kind of use. The demo I want, when you next confirm dates, is short. Three moments, with the simulation on screen:

- the rover planning a path and driving
- a sample found, and the log line that records it
- the return to the pad, and where the payment and the funders' share will sit once the test record is live

Each moment labelled simulated, logged locally, or still ahead. A booth, if you have exhibition space, would show the same three moments to people walking past. Please consider that for the next confirmed edition, once the programme for that edition is published.
